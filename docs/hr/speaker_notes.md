# Speaker Notes — HR Issue Presentation
## Employee Attrition & Talent Retention

These notes accompany each slide in [`HR_Issue_Presentation.md`](./HR_Issue_Presentation.md).
Keep them handy during the presentation. Times shown are rough guides for a 13–15 minute talk.

---

## Slide 1 — Title (0:30)

Welcome everyone. Today we will walk through a significant HR challenge we have been tracking
throughout 2024 and into 2025: a steady rise in employee attrition.

We will cover the data, root causes, and the concrete actions we are recommending. The presentation
is designed to last about 13–15 minutes, followed by open Q&A.

Please hold questions until the end if possible, but we will pause briefly after the Impact and
Root Causes slides if anything urgent comes up.

---

## Slide 2 — Executive Summary (1:00)

This slide is the "one-pager" for anyone who needs to share a summary or leave early.

Emphasise the doubling of the attrition rate—from 2.1% to 4.8%—over a single calendar year.
Make clear that we are asking for a **budget decision by March 31**. Everything else in the
presentation builds the rationale for that request.

---

## Slide 3 — Background (1:30)

Walk the audience through the timeline to show this is **not a sudden crisis**—it has been
building for over a year. Key inflection points:

- The return-to-office mandate in Q1 2023 created early friction.
- The compensation gap was identified in Q3 2023 but not acted on quickly enough.
- By Q1 2024, the first measurable attrition wave began.

The Q3 2024 HR taskforce was a positive step. Today's presentation is the output of that work,
and we now need leadership to act on the findings.

---

## Slide 4 — Current State / Problem Statement (1:30)

Be specific and factual here. **Avoid softening the numbers**—leadership needs to see the severity
in order to justify the investment.

Mention that Engineering and Sales are the hardest-hit departments because:
1. They carry the highest revenue and product-delivery impact.
2. Their roles take longest to fill (technical screening, multiple interview rounds).

The drop in the Employee Engagement Score (74 → 61) is a lagging indicator that corroborates
what we are hearing in exit interviews.

---

## Slide 5 — Impact Analysis (2:00)

This slide translates people data into **business language** that resonates with Finance and
Product leads.

Highlight the revenue risk figure ($180K ARR per open sales role per quarter). Stress that
the $561K recruitment cost in 2024 does **not** include:
- Lost productivity during vacancy periods
- Training ramp-up time for new hires (typically 60–90 days to full productivity)
- Institutional knowledge lost when senior employees depart

The true cost of attrition is therefore significantly higher than the recruitment line item alone.

---

## Slide 6 — Root Causes (1:30)

Walk through each root cause in ranked order. Compensation is both the most frequently cited
reason **and** the fastest to address operationally—making it the natural starting point.

Career visibility requires structural change (career ladders, growth conversations) but has the
highest long-term payoff for retention and internal mobility.

Hybrid-work policy is the quickest "goodwill signal" we can send to employees even before
compensation changes take effect—it costs nothing and demonstrates responsiveness.

---

## Slide 7 — Data & Evidence (1:30)

Walk through the attrition trend chart. Note that there is **no month in 2024 where attrition
meaningfully decreased**—the trend is consistently upward.

Connect each exit-interview theme back to the corresponding root cause on Slide 6. The exit
survey response rate was 89% (59 of 66 departing employees), which gives us high confidence
in these numbers.

Full raw data is available in `docs/hr/data.csv` for anyone who wants to run independent analysis.

---

## Slide 8 — Proposed Actions (2:00)

Emphasise the cost comparison:
- **$420K** salary adjustment investment
- vs. **$561K** already spent on recruiting in 2024
- vs. projected **$700K+** in 2025 if attrition continues at the current rate

The short-term actions are designed to show employees **immediate commitment**. Policy changes
and salary communications can happen within weeks and will be visible to the entire workforce.

The long-term actions build the structural foundation that prevents the problem from recurring.
Without them, salary adjustments alone will only buy time.

---

## Slide 9 — Risks & Mitigations (1:00)

Acknowledge risks honestly—leadership will ask about them anyway. Stress that the **risk of
inaction** is greater than the risk of acting.

The "attrition worsens during transition" risk deserves special attention: employees who hear
rumours of change but see no tangible action often accelerate their departure planning. Speed
of execution after budget approval is therefore critical.

---

## Slide 10 — Next Steps / Call to Action (1:00)

Make this slide extremely concrete. **No vague language.** Every item has a named owner and
a specific date.

Offer to send a follow-up email with these action items immediately after the meeting. Ask for
confirmation of ownership in the room so everyone leaves accountable.

If leadership wants to discuss the budget item offline first, suggest a 30-minute working
session with HR + Finance by March 10.

---

## Slide 11 — Appendix / Q&A (open)

Keep this slide on screen during Q&A so attendees can note the references.

**Common questions to prepare for:**

1. *"How is attrition rate calculated?"*
   Monthly: voluntary departures ÷ average headcount for that month × 100.

2. *"Is our attrition rate really worse than industry?"*
   SHRM benchmark for tech is ~1.1%/month (~13.2% annualised). We are at 4.8%/month—
   significantly above the benchmark. (Clarify: SHRM uses annual rates; we converted to
   monthly for consistency with our reporting.)

3. *"What are competitors doing?"*
   Refer to Radford report: most comparators offer 2–3 flexible remote days, structured
   promotion cycles (bi-annual), and transparent compensation bands.

4. *"Why didn't we catch this earlier?"*
   The compensation gap was flagged in Q3 2023 but not escalated to a budget decision.
   The hybrid-work friction emerged gradually. The key lesson: we need quarterly retention
   dashboards so we can act faster next time.

Thank the audience. Reiterate the **March 31 decision deadline** before closing.
